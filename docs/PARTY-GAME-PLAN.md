# Sara party game: categories and tournament

Prepared 2 October 2026 for 20–30 guests. This is a recommended event plan, not a claim that every tournament control below is already implemented. Updated for the host's preference: **open knowledge questions throughout; no yes/no, true/false or before/after guessing clues.**

## Recommended format

**Two 20-minute heats, followed by a 15-minute final; allow about 65 minutes including instructions, changeovers and prizes.** Teams contain three or four people. Exactly one team advances from each heat. The final starts at zero, with fresh clues and fresh tokens, and its winner takes the main prize.

Three 20-minute heats plus a 20-minute final is already 80 minutes before instructions and changeovers: budget roughly 90–100 minutes. Two 25–30-minute heats plus a 25–30-minute final takes roughly 85–100 minutes with overhead. Those options compete more with the party. Two shorter heats give every guest a meaningful turn without making everyone commit to the whole evening.

| Guests | Teams | Heat allocation | Primary clue turns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | Six teams: two of four, four of three | Three teams / ten people in each heat | 18 clues: six selections per team |
| 24 | Eight teams of three | Four teams / twelve people in each heat | 20 clues: five selections per team |
| 30 | Eight teams: six of four, two of three | Four teams / fifteen people in each heat | 20 clues: five selections per team |

For 21–23 guests, six teams fit the three-to-four limit. For 25–28, use seven teams and heats of three/four teams; each heat produces one winner, so their raw scores are never compared. For 29–30, use eight teams. Shuffle teams once before play, keeping couples/friend groups mixed when convenient; publish both heat line-ups before starting.

Practical timing assumptions: a 30-second answer timer, about 10–20 seconds to choose/read/judge a clue, and at most one short steal attempt. Eighteen to twenty clues average roughly 45–55 seconds each, or 14–18 minutes; the remaining time covers tokens and a tiebreak. These are rehearsal estimates, not research-established timings. If 20 clues take longer on the real TV, reduce the cap by a whole rotation of teams.

**End after a complete turn rotation.** Do not let an arbitrary time boundary give one team an extra primary selection. Announce the final rotation as the clock approaches the limit; finish that rotation and stop. Keep a short buffer inside the 20-minute slot for this. The final has twelve primary selections, six per finalist, then an optional simultaneous last question or tiebreak within its 15-minute cap.

## Turn, token and advancement rules

- The game identifies the current team automatically. That team chooses a tile, the timer starts, and its spokesperson gives the answer. Rotate the spokesperson within the team each primary turn so every person gets a voice.
- Correct: award the displayed value, reveal the answer, grey out the taken tile and move to the next primary team. Wrong: deduct the displayed value and offer the allowed steal before revealing. An unanswered/revealed tile is retired so nobody later answers an already exposed question.
- Each team has **one Double Up, one First Letter and one Steal per round**, as confirmed by the host. Double Up doubles that team's gain or loss on its declared attempt. First Letter reveals the answer's initial. Steal passes the unrevealed clue to the next eligible team, without changing the primary turn sequence. A successful steal is ordinary value. No automatic point deduction when the timer merely expires.
- Declare tokens before answer reveal; no chain of unlimited steals. A team gets at most one answer attempt per clue. Avoid answer text like “A Mojito” making First Letter accidentally reveal an article; use the meaningful answer initial.
- Exactly one heat winner advances. If tied, tied teams write a simultaneous numerical estimate on one fresh, verified question; closest wins. If equally close, use the next prepared estimate. No phones, fastest shout or alphabetical truncation. Keep two reserve tiebreak questions ready.
- Reset main scores and all three tokens for the final. Give both finalists equal primary turns. Category champions can receive fun mini-awards from a separate tally; they do not create extra finalists or more rounds.

The Lovable reference also exposes a **Joker** token: once-per-game pranks include Speed (15 seconds), Missing Word and Reverse (answer shown; guess the question); Shuffle was disabled in the inspected configuration. The confirmed three tokens above are enough for this implementation. Choose explicit Joker rules separately before adding it, especially if it changes question difficulty.

## Keep the rest of the room involved

Give nonplaying teams a one-page “play along” sheet for the same clues, with a separate audience prize. Answers stay quiet until the host reveals them. That gives spectators something to do without letting them call out answers or influence official points. It is optional: guests may also chat or visit the bar during another heat.

Explain each heat in one sentence, show who is up next, and keep Sara's celebrity reveal images for the final so the room has something new to watch. Allow a short cheer/reaction, then continue. A main winner's prize plus a light audience prize and one funniest-team-name award are enough; category mini-awards should take seconds, not another ceremony.

## Implementation gates

1. **Core play:** automatic team sequence, automatic timer, taken tiles, three one-use tokens, undo and reload restoration. Complete the four missing celebrity scenes in parallel. This is the work already requested.
2. **Event reliability:** curated tournament boards, distinct clue sets by stage, equal-turn caps, a visible round clock, a tiebreak state, exactly one advancement slot per heat and reset tokens/scores for the final. The current setup shuffles categories rather than choosing the boards above, and currently gathers all tied heat winners then truncates finalists to six in [app.js](../game/app.js); that is not the recommended tie policy. These tournament enhancements are recommendations, beyond the current turn/token implementation.
3. **Content gate:** host confirms personal stories and quotes; rehearse pictures on the actual TV, accepted answers, a wrong/steal/correct sequence and one full timed heat. Adjust difficulty by trying clues on someone who will not attend. Keep private material in the local private pack.
4. **Party run:** keep one host laptop/tab, backup private JSON, printed heat line-ups and two tiebreak questions. Use manual “End this round” as a temporary fallback while round caps/tiebreak controls remain unimplemented.

Research evidence informs the format; it does not prove that a particular category or minute count will be enjoyable for this guest list:

- [Official Pop Culture Jeopardy FAQ](https://www.jeopardy.com/be-on-j/pop-culture/faqs): uses teams of three and encourages varied knowledge within each team. This is direct quiz precedent for small teams and mixed topics.
- [Primary small-group study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2021.733663/full): a quasi-experiment with 102 schoolchildren found four-person learning gains exceeded those of individuals and pairs, but not significantly those of three-person groups. This supports treating three/four as reasonable small groups; it does not establish an adult party optimum or prove that four beats three.
- [Jeopardy producer transcript](https://www.jeopardy.com/sites/default/files/2025-01/11_25_24_Inside_J.pdf): describes Pop Culture Jeopardy's winning teams advancing through a tournament to its final. One winner per heat is our practical adaptation, avoiding comparisons between different question boards.
- [Official Jeopardy press kit](https://www.jeopardy.com/sites/default/files/2025-09/J_S42_Press_Kit_9.8.25_0.pdf): describes thirty seconds to write a Final Jeopardy response. A simultaneous last question, with spectators writing separate guesses, is our adaptation; audience enjoyment has not been experimentally established here.
